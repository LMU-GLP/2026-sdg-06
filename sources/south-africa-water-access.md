# South Africa's water access has barely improved in two decades

- **Source:** Business Day, reporting on Statistics South Africa's 2025 General Household Survey (survey period: January–December 2025)
- **URL:** https://www.businessday.co.za/economy/2026-05-27-sa-sees-little-improvement-in-piped-water-access-over-two-decades/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **87.4% of South African households had access to piped/tap water in 2025**, up from **84.4% in 2002** — a 3 percentage point increase over 23 years.
- **15.9 million households** had municipal water access in 2025, up from **9.2 million in 2004** — a 72.8% increase in household count even though the percentage barely moved (population growth absorbed most of the gain).
- **37.6% of households experienced water interruptions lasting more than 2 consecutive days (or 15 days total annually) in 2025**, up from **24.3% in 2012** — a 13.3 percentage point increase.
- Access to piped water **declined between 2002 and 2025** in four provinces: **Limpopo, Mpumalanga, Free State, and Gauteng.**
- Confidence in water safety varies sharply by province: **Limpopo highest at 92.3%; Northern Cape lowest at 64.7%.**
- Nationally, **83.5% of households reported that their drinking water was clear, colourless and free from visible sediment.**

## Summary

South Africa's headline piped-water-access number has barely moved in 23 years (84.4% to 87.4%), while the share of households living with multi-day water interruptions has risen sharply (24.3% to 37.6% since 2012) — meaning even households nominally "connected" are seeing worse service, not better, and four provinces have gone backward on access entirely.
