# Trust in Seoul's tap water reaches record high

- **Source:** Jung Min-ho, *The Korea Times*, November 10, 2025. Reporting on the Seoul Metropolitan Government's annual survey of 1,000 adults.
- **URL:** https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/society/20251110/trust-in-seouls-tap-water-reaches-record-high
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- **75% of Seoul citizens now drink Arisu**, the city's tap water — a record high, up 5.4 percentage points year over year.
- 56.3% drink it at home; 18.7% in public spaces like parks, sports facilities, and walking trails.
- Overall satisfaction with tap water: **79.9%**, up from 77.4% in 2024. Satisfaction with water *quality*: **82.2%**, up from 78.2%.
- Arisu is tested for **350 different substances**, "far surpassing both national and World Health Organization requirements."
- Measured by the U.S. definition of a tap water drinker, Seoul's rate is **80.8%, versus a U.S. average of 65%.**
- More than 70% called Arisu "world-class"; nearly 80% called it a civic asset worth protecting.

## Why the other 25% still won't drink it — the buildable part

- **71% cite concerns about old residential pipes.**
- **44.5% cite lingering doubts over filtration or safety.**
- Seoul has been replacing old pipes since 2007, targeting **more than 565,000 households**, and is **94% complete.**
- **Water purifiers are now in about half of all Korean homes.**

## Direct quotes

> "This year's results show that more than seven out of 10 Seoul citizens now consider tap water a daily beverage and trust its quality." — Lee Hoe-seung, head of Seoul Water

> "During Korea's breakneck industrialization in the 1970s and '80s, the country's tap water was widely regarded as unsafe, hobbled by aging pipes, insufficient filtration and unchecked industrial runoff that fouled its rivers. The result was deep public mistrust, and a national habit of boiling water before drinking it."

## Summary

Seoul's tap water is tested against 350 substances and beats WHO standards, yet
a quarter of residents still won't drink it and purifiers sit in half of all
Korean homes. The gap is not about water quality — it's about the pipes between
the treatment plant and the tap, and about a distrust that took root during the
1970s and 80s and outlived the problem that caused it.
