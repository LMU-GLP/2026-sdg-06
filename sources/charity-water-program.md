# charity: water — global water crisis program

- **Source:** charity: water
- **URL:** https://www.charitywater.org/global-water-crisis
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **"Nearly 1 in 10 people worldwide"** lack access to clean water, per the organization's framing of the crisis.
- **"Every $1 invested in joint water supply and sanitation provides a $4.30 economic return."**
- **Women and girls are responsible for water collection in 7 out of 10 households with water off premises.**
- **"100% of your donation"** goes to funding water projects; the organization states private donors cover operating costs separately.

## How the model works

charity: water serves isolated rural communities where residents spend hours daily collecting water. It works with local experts and community members to build wells, piped systems, BioSand Filters, or rainwater harvesting systems matched to the location, and every project includes "sanitation and hygiene training," plus the formation of a local Water Committee to keep the system running long-term.

## Direct quotes

> "Diseases from dirty water kill more people every year than all forms of violence, including war."

> "Every $1 invested in joint water supply and sanitation provides a $4.30 economic return."

## Note on what this source does not say

The page does not give a cumulative count of people served, countries active in, or total projects funded — those figures were not found and are not stated here.

## Summary

charity: water pairs community-specific water infrastructure (wells, piped systems, filters, rain harvesting) with hygiene training and a local governance committee, funded through a model where 100% of donations go directly to projects. It is one concrete example of a program built around the same access gap the UN and Our World in Data figures describe, but at the scale of individual rural communities rather than a city or nation.
