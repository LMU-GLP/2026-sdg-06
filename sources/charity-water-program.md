# charity: water — global water crisis and program model

- **Source:** charity: water
- **URL:** https://www.charitywater.org/global-water-crisis
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **Nearly 1 in 10 people worldwide lack access to clean water** — equivalent to twice the U.S. population.
- Women and girls handle water collection in **7-8 out of 10 households** without on-premise water access.
- Children under five are **over 20 times more likely** to die from unsafe water-related illness than from conflict.
- **"Every $1 invested in joint water supply and sanitation provides a $4.30 economic return."**
- **100% of public donations fund water projects**; operational costs are covered separately by private donors.

## Direct quotes

> "Diseases from dirty water kill more people every year than all forms of violence, including war."

## The model

charity: water partners with local experts to build wells, piped systems, BioSand filters, and rainwater harvesting systems, paired with a Community Water Committee and sanitation/hygiene training for each project — the water infrastructure and the local ownership structure are delivered together, not separately.

## Summary

charity: water is an existing nonprofit model for community water access: local partnerships, a $4.30-per-$1 economic return claim, and a funding structure that routes 100% of public donations directly into projects. It's the "someone is already doing this" reference point for whichever region this team picks, most directly relevant if the region has households lacking on-premise water access at all — likely a closer fit for a global/rural framing than for LA or Seoul, where the problem is efficiency and trust rather than basic access.
