# Global Water Access — Our World in Data

- **Source:** Our World in Data, "Clean Water"
- **URL:** https://ourworldindata.org/water-access
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **One in four people globally lack access to safe drinking water.**
- **Nearly three-quarters (~73%) of the world's population uses a safely managed water source.**
- Unsafe water causes **over one million deaths annually.**
- In 2015, around **70% of the global population** had safe drinking water access.
- In the lowest-income countries, **less than one-third** of the population uses a safely managed water source.
- Death rates from unsafe water often exceed **50 deaths per 100,000 people** in Sub-Saharan Africa and Asia, versus **below 0.1 deaths per 100,000 people** across Europe — a **greater than 1000-fold difference**.
- Rural populations lag behind urban areas in improved water access across nearly all countries measured.

## Note on scope

This source has no country-level figures for the United States, South Korea, or Los Angeles/California — it's global/regional-bloc data only. Don't use it to make a US-vs-Korea comparison; it can only describe the global backdrop those two sit inside.

## Summary

Roughly a quarter of people worldwide still lack safe drinking water, and the gap between rich and poor countries is stark: death rates from unsafe water are over 1000 times higher in the lowest-income regions than in Europe. This is global-scale context, not a substitute for local LA or Seoul data.
