# Drinking-water — WHO Fact Sheet

- **Source:** World Health Organization, "Drinking-water" fact sheet
- **URL:** https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/drinking-water
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **73% of the global population (6 billion people)** used safely managed drinking-water services in 2022.
- **2.2 billion people** lacked safely managed drinking-water services in 2022, broken down as:
  - **1.5 billion** with only basic service (an improved source within a 30-minute round trip).
  - **292 million** with limited service (an improved source exceeding a 30-minute round trip).
  - **296 million** using unprotected wells or springs.
  - **115 million** collecting untreated surface water directly.
- **At least 1.7 billion people** (2022) used a drinking-water source contaminated with fecal matter.
- **~505,000 diarrheal deaths annually** are attributed to microbiologically contaminated drinking water.
- **~1 million deaths yearly** from diarrhea caused by unsafe drinking water, sanitation, and hygiene combined.
- **Over 2 billion people** live in water-stressed countries as of 2021.
- **395,000 child deaths annually** (under age 5) are considered preventable through addressing water, sanitation, and hygiene risks.
- **Over 251.4 million people** required preventive treatment for schistosomiasis in 2021, a disease linked to unsafe water contact.

## Summary

WHO's global tracking shows most people now have safely managed drinking water, but a large minority — 2.2 billion — do not, and unsafe water still drives roughly a million deaths a year through diarrheal disease. Water stress (over 2 billion people, 2021) is a separate figure from the water-quality/access numbers and should not be added to them, since they measure different things.
