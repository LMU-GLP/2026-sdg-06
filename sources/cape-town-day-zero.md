# Cape Town's "Day Zero" water crisis

- **Source:** Wikipedia, "Cape Town water crisis" (compiling reporting and city data on the 2015–2020 drought and water-restriction period)
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_water_crisis
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- City population was **approximately 4.1 million by 2015**; roughly **one-fifth of Cape Town's residents** lived in informal settlements.
- Dam storage levels: **21.2% of total capacity on May 15, 2017**; **21.4% on May 14, 2018** (near the peak of the crisis).
- The city progressively cut the per-person daily water allowance: **"100 litres per person per day"** (Level 4, June 2017) → **"87 litres per day"** (Level 5, September 2017) → **"50 litres per person per day"** (Level 6B, February 2018).
- The threatened "Day Zero" shutoff scenario would have rationed residents to **"25 litres of water per person"** per day, collected from central points.
- City-wide consumption fell to **"481 million litres per day on 2 July 2018,"** against a target of **"around 500 million litres per day"** — **"more than half"** of pre-crisis consumption.
- Recovery: dam levels reached **53.6% by May 18, 2020**, and **95%** once "good rains in 2020 effectively broke the drought."

## Summary

Cape Town got within roughly three months of shutting off household taps entirely and rationing 4 million people to 25 litres of water a day, and only avoided it by cutting per-person consumption by more than half through tiered restrictions — a demand-side response, not a supply fix, since the crisis ultimately broke only when rain returned.
