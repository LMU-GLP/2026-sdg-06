# Orange County: Groundwater Replenishment System (GWRS)

## What it is

GWRS, run jointly by the Orange County Water District and OC San (Orange County Sanitation District), takes treated wastewater and purifies it through a three-step process — **microfiltration, reverse osmosis, and ultraviolet light treatment** — before injecting or percolating it back into Orange County's groundwater basin ([OCWD](../sources/ocwd-groundwater-replenishment.md)).

OCWD frames it simply: **"NEW WATER YOU CAN COUNT ON"** ([OCWD](../sources/ocwd-groundwater-replenishment.md)).

## Scale

- Current capacity: **130 million gallons per day (MGD)**.
- Of that: **30 MGD** goes to seawater intrusion barrier injection wells (Huntington Beach & Fountain Valley), **10 MGD** to Mid-Basin injection wells (Santa Ana), and **90 MGD** to percolation basins (Anaheim).
- Cumulative production reported by OCWD: **506.88 billion gallons** (through end of June 2026).

(All figures: [OCWD](../sources/ocwd-groundwater-replenishment.md).)

## What we don't know from this source

The pages read did not give an acre-feet-per-year conversion, the number of people served, a start date, or cost. OCWD points to a technical brochure and its 2024 annual report for that — not yet in this wiki's sources.

## Why it's relevant to the challenge

GWRS is a working, operating example of "use water more wisely" applied to wastewater rather than stormwater: instead of treating and discharging it, the water is purified to a higher standard and put back into the supply. It's a regional-infrastructure-scale program, in contrast to LADWP's household/neighborhood-scale stormwater interventions — see [Los Angeles: Stormwater Capture](los-angeles-stormwater.md). Both are Southern California, but a different agency (Orange County Water District vs. the City of LA's LADWP) and a different water source (wastewater vs. stormwater); this wiki does not currently have a source establishing the two systems interact or share infrastructure.
