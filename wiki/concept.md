# Concept

## What it is

An **acoustic leak-detection product for municipal water utilities**: sensors clamped onto the pipe network that listen for the sound signature of underground leaks, paired with monitoring software that shows the utility where treated water is being lost before it reaches households.

## Who it's for

**Municipal water utilities, not households** — specifically, we're starting with **Cape Town's** municipal water utility as the pilot customer.

## Why Cape Town, and why municipalities first

Our [problem statement](./problem-statement.md) shows South Africa's water crisis concentrated hardest in provinces like Mpumalanga (36.0% weekly interruptions) and Limpopo (25.9%), which have less municipal budget than a metro like Cape Town (0.3% weekly interruptions, 74.8% of households with no interruptions at all) ([Water interruptions by province](../sources/sa-water-interruptions-by-province.md)). Selling hardware-plus-subscription to whoever can pay would mean serving the municipalities that need it least by our own evidence.

We're doing it anyway, on purpose, for two reasons visible in our own sources:

1. **The 2025 survey itself names Cape Town** — not just the poorer inland provinces — as one of two metros (with Johannesburg) most likely to see prolonged outages today, as metro-wide interruptions have risen from 7% of households in 2015 to over 30% now ([Water interruptions by province](../sources/sa-water-interruptions-by-province.md)). So there's a real, documented problem in Cape Town, not just a convenient budget.
2. **Cape Town already has a track record of taking water management seriously** — it cut consumption by more than half to avert Day Zero in 2017–2018 ([Cape Town Day Zero](../sources/cape-town-day-zero.md)) — which makes it a faster sale to prove the product works, before trying to bring it to municipalities with tighter budgets and less institutional appetite for this kind of investment.

## How it works in the real world

- Acoustic sensors are installed at points across Cape Town's municipal pipe network.
- The sensors continuously listen for the sound signature of leaks and feed data to a monitoring dashboard.
- The utility pays for the hardware up front (sale or lease) plus an ongoing subscription for the monitoring software and data analysis.
- The utility acts on what the dashboard flags — repairing or prioritizing the highest-loss sections of pipe first.

## What we're deliberately leaving out (for now)

- **Households, entirely.** This version has no consumer product, no household app, no direct household outreach. If a household-facing product happens, it's a later phase, not this one.
- **The hardest-hit provinces.** Mpumalanga, Limpopo, Free State, and other lower-budget, worse-affected areas are not part of this first version, even though our own evidence says that's where the interruption problem is most severe. We are consciously starting where we can prove the product works, not where the need is greatest — and we're saying so plainly rather than implying this version already solves the worst of the problem.

## Open questions this concept doesn't answer yet

- No source in this wiki tells us what share of Cape Town's specific water loss is attributable to leaks versus other causes (e.g. non-revenue water from theft, metering error, or demand outpacing supply) — so we don't yet know how much of Cape Town's interruption problem this product could actually fix.
- No source gives Cape Town's current non-revenue water rate or existing leak-detection infrastructure, so we don't know how much of this problem the city may already be addressing.
