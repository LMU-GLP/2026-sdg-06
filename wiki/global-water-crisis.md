# The global water crisis, in figures

The UN frames the goal in absolute numbers: **2.1 billion people lack safely managed drinking water, 3.4 billion lack safely managed sanitation, and 1.7 billion lack basic hygiene services** ([UN SDG 6 targets](../sources/un-sdg6-targets.md)).

Our World in Data's aggregation of WHO/UNICEF figures puts the drinking-water share of that gap this way: **"Almost three-quarters of the world's population uses a safely managed water source. One in four people does not use a safe drinking water source."** Progress has been slow — **around 70% of the global population had safe drinking water in 2015**, the SDG baseline year, versus roughly 75% now ([Global water access data](../sources/global-water-access-data.md)).

charity: water frames the same gap at household scale: **"nearly 1 in 10 people worldwide"** lack access to clean water, by their count ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

**Note on these three figures:** "one in four" (OWID/JMP), "nearly 1 in 10" (charity: water), and the UN's 2.1 billion are not the same measurement. They come from different organizations, may use different definitions of "access," and are not restated here as a single agreed number — see [Data note: why these figures don't match](#data-note-why-these-figures-dont-match) below. This wiki keeps all three rather than averaging them, per the project's sourcing rules.

## Who this falls on hardest

- In the lowest-income countries, **fewer than one-third of the population** accesses safely managed water, concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa ([Global water access data](../sources/global-water-access-data.md)).
- **Unsafe water causes more than one million deaths a year globally**, and the death-rate gap between high-income Europe (below 0.1 per 100,000) and Sub-Saharan Africa/Asia (often above 50 per 100,000) is **"greater than 1000-fold"** ([Global water access data](../sources/global-water-access-data.md)).
- **Women and girls are responsible for water collection in 7 out of 10 households** where water isn't on the premises ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).
- Urban areas have higher water access than rural areas in nearly every country measured ([Global water access data](../sources/global-water-access-data.md)).

## Why it's worth fixing, economically

**"Every $1 invested in joint water supply and sanitation provides a $4.30 economic return"** ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

## Data note: why these figures don't match

Per this wiki's sourcing rule, figures from different sources measuring different things are not compared or averaged. The UN's 2.1 billion (safely managed drinking water gap), OWID's "one in four" (safe drinking water source), and charity: water's "nearly 1 in 10" (clean water access, framing unspecified) likely draw on different definitions and possibly different years. Treat each as that source's own framing, not as three confirmations of the same number.
