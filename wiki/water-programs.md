# Programs already working on this

## charity: water

charity: water builds community-specific water infrastructure — wells, piped systems, BioSand Filters, or rainwater harvesting systems, chosen per location — for isolated rural communities where residents currently spend hours a day collecting water. Every project pairs the infrastructure with "sanitation and hygiene training" and the formation of a local Water Committee meant to keep the system running after the organization leaves ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

Its funding model: **"100% of your donation"** goes to water projects, with the organization stating that operating costs are covered separately by private donors ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

Its case for why this is worth funding: **"Every $1 invested in joint water supply and sanitation provides a $4.30 economic return,"** and **"diseases from dirty water kill more people every year than all forms of violence, including war"** ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

The source does not give a cumulative total of people served, countries active in, or number of projects funded — that figure was not found, so it is not stated here ([charity: water program](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

## LADWP's rebate and parks programs (Los Angeles)

LADWP itself runs two smaller-scale programs worth noting as precedent for a student-built solution: a **rain barrel rebate through SoCal Water$mart**, and a **Stormwater Capture Parks Program** collecting runoff at nine parks in the East San Fernando Valley ([LA stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md)).

## Seoul's pipe-replacement program (South Korea)

Seoul has been replacing old residential pipes since 2007, targeting **more than 565,000 households**, and is **94% complete** ([Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)). This is a government infrastructure program, not a business or nonprofit, but it's the direct intervention behind Seoul's rising trust numbers — see [South Korea: water](./south-korea-water.md).
