# Los Angeles vs. South Korea (Seoul)

Neither region has been chosen yet. This page puts the two problems side by side — **the figures below are not measuring the same thing and should not be averaged or converted into each other** (per this wiki's rules).

| | Los Angeles | Seoul, South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| **The core problem** | Supply/drought — a drainage system built to dump rainwater into the ocean instead of keeping it ([source](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md)) | Trust, not supply — water tested to 350 substances, exceeding WHO standards, yet a quarter of residents won't drink it ([source](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)) |
| **A key figure** | Captures **more than 83,000 acre-feet/year (27+ billion gallons)** of stormwater | **75%** of citizens drink tap water (Arisu), up 5.4 points year over year |
| **What's already fixed / underway** | Existing rebates and a 9-park capture program (see [existing-solutions.md](existing-solutions.md)) | Pipe replacement program, 94% complete since 2007 |
| **What's left to solve** | Expanding household/neighborhood capture (rain barrels, rain gardens, permeable paving) | Closing a trust gap that outlived its original cause (71% still cite old-pipe concerns even as replacement nears completion) |
| **Nature of the fix** | Physical/infrastructure + adoption of existing rebates | Communication/behavior change — the infrastructure fix is nearly done |

## Why these numbers can't be compared directly

- LA's figure (acre-feet of stormwater captured) and Seoul's figure (% of residents drinking tap water) measure entirely different things — one is a supply volume, the other is a behavior/trust rate. There is no shared unit between them.
- The one place a direct US-vs-Korea number appears is inside the Seoul source itself: "Measured by the U.S. definition of a tap water drinker, Seoul's rate is 80.8%, versus a U.S. average of 65%" ([source](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)). This is *that source's* comparison, not verified independently in this wiki — flag it as such if it's used in a pitch.
- Neither region appears in the [global dataset source](../sources/owid-water-access-data.md), which only has global/regional-bloc figures — so global statistics can't be used to adjudicate between LA and Seoul either.

## What would help decide

- A directly comparable figure — e.g., water lost/wasted as a % of total available supply in each region — isn't in `sources/` yet for either region. Right now the comparison is apples to oranges: LA has a capture-capacity number, Seoul has a trust-rate number, and nothing in this wiki puts them on the same scale.
