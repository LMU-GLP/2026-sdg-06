# Existing programs and solutions

## charity: water — community water access, global

Partners with local experts to build wells, piped systems, BioSand filters, and rainwater harvesting, paired with a Community Water Committee and sanitation/hygiene training per project. Claims **"every $1 invested in joint water supply and sanitation provides a $4.30 economic return"**, and routes **100% of public donations** directly into projects, with operational costs covered separately by private donors ([charity: water](../sources/charity-water-program.md)).

**Fit check:** this model targets communities with **no on-premise water access at all** — the "1 in 10 people worldwide" case. Neither LA nor Seoul is that case (see [global-water-crisis.md](global-water-crisis.md)), so this is a reference point for the *model* (infrastructure + local ownership delivered together), not a program to imitate directly in either region.

## LADWP rebate and capture programs — Los Angeles

SoCal Water$mart rain barrel rebate, plus a Stormwater Capture Parks Program already running at nine East San Fernando Valley parks ([LADWP](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md)). This is a government utility program already operating at the exact household/neighborhood scale a prototype could plug into or extend — e.g., a tool that helps a resident find and use the existing rebate, rather than a competing program.

## Seoul pipe replacement — South Korea

City-run program replacing old residential pipes since 2007, targeting over 565,000 households, now 94% complete ([Korea Times](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)). The infrastructure fix is nearly finished; no source here describes an existing program addressing the *trust* gap specifically — that looks like the open space for a team solution in this region.
