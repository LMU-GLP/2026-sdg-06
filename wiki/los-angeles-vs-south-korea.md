# Los Angeles vs. South Korea

These two regions have a source apiece, and the sources aren't measuring the same thing. **This page does not convert one region's figures into the other's terms** — per this wiki's sourcing rules, a figure from one country is not compared to a figure from another unless both measure the same thing the same way, and here they don't.

## What each region's problem actually is

| | Los Angeles | South Korea (Seoul) |
|---|---|---|
| Core problem | A drainage system built to send rainwater to the ocean, not capture it ([LA stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md)) | Tap water that meets or beats safety standards but that a quarter of residents still won't drink ([Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)) |
| The number that defines it | **83,000+ acre-feet/year (27+ billion gallons)** captured; more than that is lost to the ocean | **75% drink Arisu**; the other ~25% mostly cite pipes (71%) or filtration doubts (44.5%) |
| What's broken | Infrastructure and design | Trust and aging residential pipes |
| Is it a supply problem or a perception problem? | Supply — the water is there when it rains, the capacity to keep it isn't | Perception and pipe-age — the water already meets standards |

## Why they aren't directly comparable

- LA's figure is a **volume** (acre-feet/gallons of stormwater captured per year). Korea's figures are **survey percentages** (share of residents who drink tap water, or who cite a specific concern). There's no shared unit to put them on one chart.
- LA's source is an agency describing infrastructure capacity. Korea's source is a citizen survey. These measure different things: physical capacity vs. stated behavior/trust.
- The one head-to-head number in either source — **Seoul's 80.8% vs. the U.S. average of 65%**, using the U.S. definition of a tap water drinker — is a comparison the Korea source makes itself, using one consistent definition. It is not this wiki's own construction, and it compares Seoul to a national U.S. average, not to Los Angeles specifically ([Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)).

## What this means for picking a region

- **Los Angeles** has an infrastructure/engineering problem with clearly buildable small-scale interventions already named by the utility: rain barrels, permeable pavers, rain gardens, bioswales ([LA stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md)).
- **South Korea** has a behavioral/trust problem where the physical fix (pipe replacement) is already 94% done, and what's left is closing a communication and legacy-distrust gap ([Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)).

These are genuinely different kinds of problems — one is "help people capture and reuse more water," the other is "help people trust water that's already safe." Neither source gives evidence for which is the bigger opportunity; that's a team decision, not a data one.
