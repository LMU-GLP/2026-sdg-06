# Los Angeles vs. South Korea (Seoul): Comparing Before We Choose

The two regional sources in this wiki describe **different kinds of water problems**, measured in **different units**. This page lays them side by side without forcing them into a single number — per this wiki's rules, figures from different measurement types don't get compared or averaged.

## Los Angeles: a supply-capture problem

- Source: [LADWP stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md), see also [wiki page](los-angeles-stormwater.md).
- Measured in: **volume of water captured vs. lost** (acre-feet/gallons per year).
- The core fact: LADWP can capture **more than 83,000 acre-feet (27+ billion gallons) per year**, and everything above that capacity is discharged to the Pacific Ocean, because the storm drain system was built against flooding, not drought.
- The fix is physical and buildable: rain barrels, permeable pavers, rain gardens, bioswales, infiltration basins.

## Seoul: a trust and distribution problem

- Source: [Korea Times, tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md), see also [wiki page](seoul-tap-water-trust.md).
- Measured in: **self-reported behavior and satisfaction** (% of citizens drinking tap water, % satisfied), from a survey of 1,000 adults.
- The core fact: **75% of Seoul citizens drink Arisu** (their tap water), despite water quality already exceeding WHO standards (tested for 350 substances). The remaining ~25% cite **old residential pipes (71%)** and **filtration/safety doubts (44.5%)** — not current water quality.
- The fix is behavioral and infrastructural-legacy: pipe replacement (94% complete on a 565,000+ household program) and rebuilding trust that outlived the 1970s–80s conditions that caused it.

## Why these numbers can't be compared directly

- LA's figure is a **physical capacity measurement** (how much water infrastructure can hold). Seoul's figure is a **perception survey result** (how many people say they drink the water). These don't measure the same thing, so no ratio or "LA captures X, Seoul only trusts Y%" comparison is valid — that would be comparing volume to opinion.
- Neither source gives the other region's equivalent metric: this wiki has no stormwater-capture figures for Seoul, and no tap-water-trust survey for LA. Filling that gap would need new sources, not extrapolation from what's here.

## What this suggests for choosing a region

The two problems are structurally different:

- **LA** is a "we're wasting water we could keep" problem — an engineering/capture gap, addressable at household/neighborhood scale (see [Los Angeles: Stormwater Capture](los-angeles-stormwater.md)).
- **Seoul** is a "the water's fine but people don't believe it" problem — a trust/legacy-infrastructure gap, addressable through communication, pipe replacement tracking, or in-home verification tools.

A third data point in this wiki, [Orange County's GWRS](water-reuse-orange-county.md), is a regional-infrastructure-scale wastewater reuse program — closer in kind to LA's capture problem than to Seoul's trust problem, though it addresses a different water source (wastewater, not stormwater).

Either region — or another one entirely — is workable; the sources here just establish that "protect clean water and use it more wisely" means something different in each place, so the team's prototype should match the problem type it's actually solving, not assume the two are interchangeable.
