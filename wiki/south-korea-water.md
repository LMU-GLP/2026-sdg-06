# South Korea: tap water trust

Seoul's water problem, per *The Korea Times*' report on the city's annual survey, isn't quality — it's trust that hasn't caught up to quality. Arisu, the city's tap water, is **"tested for 350 different substances,"** which the source says is **"far surpassing both national and World Health Organization requirements"** ([Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)).

## Key figures

- **75% of Seoul citizens now drink Arisu** — a record high, up 5.4 percentage points year over year. 56.3% drink it at home; 18.7% in public spaces (parks, sports facilities, walking trails).
- Overall satisfaction with tap water: **79.9%** (up from 77.4% in 2024). Satisfaction with water *quality* specifically: **82.2%** (up from 78.2%).
- Measured by the U.S. definition of a tap water drinker, Seoul's rate is **80.8%, versus a U.S. average of 65%** — a comparison the source itself makes, using one consistent definition across both.
- More than 70% called Arisu "world-class"; nearly 80% called it a civic asset worth protecting.

(All figures: [Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md).)

## Why the remaining ~25% still won't drink it

- **71% cite concerns about old residential pipes.**
- **44.5% cite lingering doubts over filtration or safety.**
- Seoul has been replacing old pipes since 2007, targeting **more than 565,000 households**, and is **94% complete**.
- **Water purifiers are now in about half of all Korean homes** — a habit that predates and outlasts the infrastructure fix.

The source traces the mistrust to a specific historical origin: **"During Korea's breakneck industrialization in the 1970s and '80s, the country's tap water was widely regarded as unsafe, hobbled by aging pipes, insufficient filtration and unchecked industrial runoff that fouled its rivers. The result was deep public mistrust, and a national habit of boiling water before drinking it."** ([Korea tap water trust](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md))
