# Los Angeles: stormwater capture

LA's core water problem, per LADWP, is a design problem, not a supply problem: **"Historically in Los Angeles, the storm drain systems are designed to quickly direct rainfall and runoff from impervious surfaces into the storm drain system and ultimately the Pacific Ocean. The system was designed to protect life and property from flood impacts."** ([LA stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md))

## Key figures

- LADWP's stormwater capture facilities can currently capture **more than 83,000 acre-feet per year (more than 27 billion gallons)** under average conditions.
- Centralized "spreading grounds" facilities alone account for **more than 41,000 acre-feet (more than 13.3 billion gallons)** per year of that.
- Spreading grounds at Hansen, Tujunga, Pacoima, and Lopez recharge the San Fernando Groundwater Basin.
- Whatever exceeds that capture capacity is lost: **"Stormwater runoff that cannot be contained at these facilities is discharged to the Pacific Ocean via streams, rivers, and storm drains."**

(All figures: [LA stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md).)

## What's buildable at neighborhood/household scale

LADWP names specific small-scale interventions: **rain barrels and cisterns, permeable pavers, rain gardens, bioswales, infiltration swales, and infiltration basins** beneath street medians and parkways. LADWP also runs a rain barrel rebate through SoCal Water$mart, and a Stormwater Capture Parks Program collecting runoff at nine parks in the East San Fernando Valley ([LA stormwater capture](../sources/la-stormwater-capture.md)).

This matters for a student prototype specifically because these are interventions at a scale a program, app, or campaign could plausibly influence — unlike, say, the capacity of a spreading ground.
