# South Korea: Seoul's tap water trust gap

## The water quality is not the problem

Seoul's tap water (Arisu) is tested for **350 different substances**, "far surpassing both national and World Health Organization requirements" ([Korea Times / Seoul Water survey](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)). Satisfaction with water quality specifically is **82.2%**, up from 78.2% the prior year.

## But a quarter of residents still won't drink it

- **75% of Seoul citizens now drink Arisu** — a record high, up 5.4 percentage points year over year. By the U.S. definition of a tap water drinker, Seoul's rate is **80.8%, versus a U.S. average of 65%** — though note this is one source's own cross-national comparison, not independently verified against how the U.S. figure was measured.
- Of the roughly 25% who still don't drink it: **71% cite concerns about old residential pipes**, and **44.5% cite lingering doubts over filtration or safety**.
- **Water purifiers are now in about half of all Korean homes** — a parallel, redundant system existing alongside water the city says already exceeds WHO standards.

## Where the distrust comes from

> "During Korea's breakneck industrialization in the 1970s and '80s, the country's tap water was widely regarded as unsafe, hobbled by aging pipes, insufficient filtration and unchecked industrial runoff that fouled its rivers. The result was deep public mistrust, and a national habit of boiling water before drinking it." ([Korea Times](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md))

## What's already being done

Seoul has been replacing old pipes since 2007, targeting **more than 565,000 households**, and is **94% complete** ([Korea Times](../sources/korea-tap-water-trust.md)).

## Why this is a strong prototype fit

The infrastructure fix (pipe replacement) is already 94% done and isn't a team's to build. What's left is a **trust and communication gap that outlived the problem that caused it** — that's a behavior-change/information product a student team could actually prototype (e.g., a way to show residents their specific pipe/building has already been replaced), not a civil-engineering one.
